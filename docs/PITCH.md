# RPS1 — Session 1 Pitch

Three minutes, spoken. If more than one of you presents, write a name next
to each beat. Anything in [square brackets] is a real detail only you can
fill in. Don't invent it, and don't skip it.

## The script

### 1. The person (~45s)

This is about my mother. She is the chairperson of a farmers' cooperative,
and the co-op owns a rice mill. Farmers bring in their palay, which is
unhusked rice, and she is the one who has to watch every single load. From the moment it arrives, through drying and milling,
until the customer pays and takes their rice home.

I've watched her do it. [One real moment you saw. For example: the evening
she sat with the notebook trying to work out whether a customer had already
paid.] She sees the loads with her own eyes. But the records don't keep up
with them.

### 2. Today (~30s)

Right now, everything starts in a paper notebook at intake. The rest lives
in her memory, on loose slips, and in messages to staff. When she needs to
know where a load is, or whether it's been paid, she flips pages, asks
around, and checks it all by hand. [How often this happens, or how long it
takes her.] That costs her time every day, and it costs the mill money.

### 3. Our fix (~45s)

So we're building RPS1. It's a simple phone app that lets my mother follow
every load from the first step to payment.

She types in the customer's name, the sacks, the kilos, the price, and the
service. A package deal, harvesting and drying, drying only, or milling
only. The app then shows only the steps that service has. As the work
happens, she taps the load forward.

If the customer wants to leave their palay or rice at the mill, she taps
store. Palay keeps three months. Rice keeps one. The app warns her before
anything goes bad.

One list shows every load, its step, whether it's paid, and what's about
to expire. No flipping pages. No asking around twice. And it has a login,
so her customers' details stay private.

### 4. The demo moment (~30s)

In Session 3, we'll show it live, on a phone. We'll log a package deal and
tap it from harvesting, to drying, to milling, to ready. We'll store the
rice and watch the expiry date appear. Then we'll mark it paid. That's her
whole job with one load, start to finish, in about a minute.

### 5. How we'll know (~30s)

My mother will use it on real loads before Session 3. Our test is simple:
would she rather use this than her notebook? We'll come back and tell you
exactly what she said, including what she didn't like.

## The three hardest questions

**1. "What does 'extra time and money' actually mean? How many loads, and
how much is lost?"**
This is our weakest point. `docs/PROBLEM.md` names the pain but has no
numbers and no specific thing going wrong.
*Honest answer:* "We haven't measured it yet. This week we're counting how
many loads come in per day, how long she spends checking records, and any
time a payment was missed or a load was mixed up."
**Do this before you present.** Ask your mother tonight: how many loads come
in on a busy day, and when did a load or a payment last slip through? One
real number or one real story will make beats 1 and 2 much stronger.

**2. "Staff still don't use the app. Won't she still have to ask them what
stage a load is at?"**
*Honest answer:* "Yes, for now she still hears it from staff. The
difference is she records it once, with one tap, and the answer stays in
one place. She doesn't have to ask again or hunt through slips later.
Staff accounts are the first thing on our Later list."

**3. "Why not just a better notebook, or a shared spreadsheet?"**
*Honest answer:* "A spreadsheet could hold the same data. But on a phone,
in the middle of a busy mill, typing into tiny cells is slow. Our app does
one job: log a load, tap it forward, mark it paid. If my mother says a
spreadsheet would have been enough, we'll tell you that too."
